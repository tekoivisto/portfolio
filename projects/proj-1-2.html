1.2Predicting Mechanical Properties of Nanopillars by Interpretable Machine Learning

May 2023 – May 2024
Collaborators: Prof. Lasse Laurson, Ph.D. Marcin Mińkowski
HPC, Python, Tensorflow, LAMMPS

Neural networks are applied to solve increasingly complex problems. However, the improvements in performance are achieved with increasingly complex neural networks, turning them into "black-box models" that are not interpretable by humans. In materials science, machine learning models have proven to be powerful tools for discovering links between microstructure and material properties, but the black-box nature of these models limits the physical insights one might gain from them. In this project conducted at the Tampere University Computational Physics Laboratory Complex Systems research group, I studied the mechanical properties of nanopillars using convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and gained insights to the predictions of the black-box model using interpretation techniques. I also wrote my bachelor’s thesis on this project, which led to a first-author publication in APL Machine Learning [1].

The computational study is schematically presented in Figure 1.2. First, a dataset of 5000 polycrystalline nanopillars and their corresponding stress–strain curves during deformation is generated using molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. The dataset is used to train a CNN to predict the Young’s moduli and yield stresses of the nanopillars. Different representations of the initial atomic structure are used as input to the CNN to study how much information about the mechanical properties each representation contains. Finally, the internal workings of the otherwise black-box CNN predictions are interpreted with gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM).
